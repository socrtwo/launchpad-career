# Product principles

## What this is

A career-planning and employment-guidance tool for undergraduates, graduate students,
recent graduates, and career changers.

## What this is not

Not a mental-health service. Not a diagnostic tool. Not a source of medical,
psychological, or legal advice. Not a job board, and not a promise of employment.

The "Something else" flow exists because people arrive uncertain. Uncertainty about a
career is an ordinary condition of being twenty-two, not a symptom. The product must never
respond to it as though it were one.

## Principles

1. **One goal at a time.** The home screen asks for a single choice and says plainly that
   the others remain available. A person who has to rank six priorities before they get
   any help has been given homework, not help.

2. **Say what happens next.** Every screen ends with a concrete next step. Placeholder
   screens in this build state that they are placeholders and describe the planned flow;
   they do not simulate progress that does not exist.

3. **Show the reasoning.** Any suggestion carries its basis. See
   `career-recommendation-principles.md`.

4. **Never guarantee an outcome.** No claim about admission, hiring, salary, or success.
   This is enforced by a test over the goal catalogue copy, not just by review.

5. **Ask for the minimum.** Every question must earn its place by changing the advice
   given. If an answer would not change the output, it is not asked.

6. **Multiple paths.** Where a recommendation is possible at all, more than one is offered,
   including routes that do not require a four-year or graduate degree.

7. **The user can correct us.** Any inference the product makes must be visible and
   editable. Nothing is inferred silently and then relied on.

8. **Accessible is not a phase.** Keyboard operation, semantics, contrast, text scaling,
   and reduce-motion are part of the definition of done for every screen, and are tested.

9. **Anonymous by default.** Exploration requires no account and no name.

10. **Honest about being early.** The interface says what is real and what is planned. A
    student making a decision deserves to know which is which.

## Success signals

Worth measuring later, in aggregate and without identifying anyone: whether people finish
a plan, whether they come back, and whether they change their selection after seeing the
reasoning — the last being a sign the explanations are doing work rather than decorating.

Explicitly not a success signal: time on screen.
