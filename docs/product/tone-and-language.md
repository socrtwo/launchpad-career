# Tone and language

## The register

Write the way a good adviser talks: plain, specific, and on the reader's side. Contemporary
without slang, warm without performance.

Avoid, in order of how badly each one lands:

- **Patronising.** "Don't worry!" "You've got this!" Encouragement the reader did not ask
  for reads as a substitute for help.
- **Clinical.** "Presenting concerns", "intervention", "client". This is not a clinic.
- **Bureaucratic.** "Utilise the aforementioned resources to facilitate outcomes."
- **Trendy.** Anything that will be dated by the time a sophomore graduates.
- **Academic.** Long noun phrases, passive voice, hedging stacked three deep.

## Rules

1. **Active voice, second person.** "Compare programs, costs, and requirements", not
   "Programs may be compared."
2. **A control says what it does.** "Continue", not "Submit". The word survives the whole
   flow: what "Send" sends, the confirmation calls sent.
3. **Sentence case everywhere.** Headings, buttons, labels.
4. **No labelling the person.** Never "if you're feeling lost", "confused students",
   "the anxious job seeker". Describe the situation, not the reader.
5. **Uncertainty is a situation, not a diagnosis.** The bottom-of-page entry says "Tell us
   what is on your mind, including if you feel stuck or are not sure where to begin." It
   does not offer to fix the reader.
6. **No outcome promises.** No "land the job", "guarantee", "ensures you get". Enforced by
   a test in `packages/launchpad_data/test`.
7. **Errors explain and instruct.** What happened, and what to do. They do not apologise
   and they are never vague. "We could not load the options just now. Check your connection
   and try again."
8. **Empty states invite action**, they do not editorialise about emptiness.
9. **No emoji in the product interface.** Screen readers read them aloud verbatim, they
   render differently on every platform, and they cannot be recoloured for contrast. Use
   the icon set.
10. **Numbers carry their source and date**, or they do not appear.

## Worked examples

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| "Fix my resume" | "Improve my resume and LinkedIn" |
| "I'm stuck / not sure where to start" | "Something else" |
| "Prep me for interviews" | "Prepare for interviews" |
| "Build skills that get hired" | "Build skills employers are looking for" |
| "Skills that will land you a job" | "Skills employers are looking for" |
| "Oops! Something went wrong." | "We could not load the options just now. Check your connection and try again." |

The left column is not wrong English; it is wrong for a product that also has to be
credible to a careers office and readable by someone using a screen reader. "Fix my resume"
tells the reader their resume is broken. "Land you a job" is a promise no software can
make.

## Reading level

Aim for something a first-year student reads without effort and a graduate student does not
find condescending. Short sentences do most of that work. Do not simplify vocabulary that
carries real meaning — "labour-market data" is clearer than "job info".
